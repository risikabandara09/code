Travelling Microscope — Physics Theory & Principles

1. Principle of the Travelling Microscope

A travelling microscope is a precision compound microscope mounted on a dual-axis carriage (horizontal and vertical) that can be moved with millimeter graduated main scales and sub-millimeter vernier scales. It is widely used to measure small dimensions with an accuracy of 0.01 mm (0.001 cm) without damaging delicate specimens.

2. Least Count (LC) Formula

1 Main Scale Division (MSD) = 0.5 mm = 0.05 cm
50 Vernier Scale Divisions (VSD) = 49 MSD = 24.5 mm
1 VSD = 24.5 / 50 = 0.49 mm
Least Count (LC) = 1 MSD - 1 VSD = 0.5 mm - 0.49 mm = 0.01 mm (0.001 cm)

3. Calculating Total Reading

Total Reading = MSR + (VSR × Least Count)
Example: If MSR = 32.0 mm and 47th vernier division coincides with a main scale line:
Total = 32.0 + (47 × 0.01) = 32.47 mm = 3.247 cm

4. Capillary Tube Bore Measurement

Align the vertical crosswire with the left inside edge ($R_1$), then move horizontally to the right inside edge ($R_2$).

Internal Diameter: d = |R₂ - R₁|
Internal Radius: r = d / 2

5. Refractive Index (μ) of Glass Slab

Based on the principle of Real and Apparent Depth:

R₁ = Reading focused on ink dot on paper without slab
R₂ = Reading focused on ink dot through glass slab (apparent position)
R₃ = Reading focused on lycopodium powder on top surface of slab
Refractive Index μ = (R₃ - R₁) / (R₃ - R₂) = Real Thickness / Apparent Thickness

• ↑ / ↓ : Move Vertical pillar fine adjustment
